# Product Naming and Visual Identity Brief

## Product concept

A developer-facing utility for maintaining messy Git working trees and
filesystem directories, especially a tracked home directory.

The product helps users inspect accumulated changes and decide what
should happen to each item. It is not a blunt "delete untracked files"
tool.

The broader workflow may include:

-   Reviewing modified and untracked files.
-   Finding forgotten work-in-progress changes.
-   Deciding whether to commit, stash, keep, ignore, or delete items.
-   Discovering cache directories and generated or temporary files.
-   Removing unwanted caches when appropriate.
-   Helping users add appropriate ignore rules for cache and generated
    directories.
-   Maintaining order across repositories and ordinary directories,
    including hidden files and dotfiles.

The guiding principle is **inspect, understand, then act**. Preserve
useful work; don't assume unfamiliar files are disposable.

## Naming direction

### What the name should feel like

The desired name is a memorable product name: short, distinctive, easy
to say, and a little mischievous or unconventional. It does not need to
mention Git, repositories, cleanup, or dotfiles explicitly.

The original idea was **Glustered**, a play on "flustered" with a G
suggesting Git. **Flusterd** and **Blusterd** were also considered as
shorter-sounding variations. The appeal was the invented-word quality
and the feeling of accumulated clutter, but the name should not feel
awkwardly long.

The exploration then moved away from conventional developer-tool names
and toward playful animal imagery---especially cats, lolcats, kittens,
and a cat chasing dust bunnies. The product can have personality and
humour rather than sounding like a serious corporate utility.

### Naming preferences

-   Prefer an original, brandable word or an unexpected everyday word.
-   Short and punchy is better than a long compound.
-   A little odd, funny, or internet-native is welcome.
-   The name can evoke mess, leftovers, hidden things, rummaging,
    hunting, or putting things in order.
-   Cat-related wordplay is worth exploring, but the name should still
    work as a real application name.
-   Avoid generic combinations built from obvious terms such as Git,
    Repo, Tidy, Sweep, Clean, Tree, Diff, Stash, or Commit.
-   Avoid names that feel like generic productivity software or rely on
    explaining a weak pun.

### Naming ideas explored

These were discussed, not necessarily approved:

-   **Glustered / Flusterd / Blusterd** --- the original invented-word
    direction.
-   **Purrge** --- a cat + purge pun; an existing similarly named
    cleanup app was found, but the spelling and market overlap were
    considered separately.
-   **Greebl / Greeble** --- inspired by cats chasing imaginary or
    mysterious little things; a possible metaphor for hidden filesystem
    pests.
-   **Mouser** --- a cat hunting small unwanted things.
-   **Dustpurr** --- a cat and dust-bunny combination.
-   **Blep / Mlem** --- lolcat/internet-cat vocabulary.
-   **Trashpanda** --- raccoon energy: rummaging through accumulated
    debris.

No final product name has been selected. Treat these as creative
directions and candidates, not cleared names. Any finalist should be
checked for existing software, repository/package names, domains, and
relevant trademarks.

## Visual identity and mascot

### Core concept

A mischievous **orange or ginger cat**, possibly a fluffy Persian cat,
hunting and pinning a dust bunny.

The central visual is a large orange cat paw coming down from the
upper-left and visibly grabbing or pinning a fluffy dust bunny
underneath it. The dust bunny should clearly sit below the paw, not
beside it or in front of it.

The image should feel like a playful comic illustration or
lolcat-inspired mascot, not a realistic pet photograph.

### App icon direction

The preferred composition is the **large icon from the left-hand
concept** in the earlier set:

-   A large ginger/orange cat paw enters from the upper-left.
-   The paw visibly presses down on a dust bunny.
-   The dust bunny is underneath the paw and remains clearly readable.
-   Use a warm beige or pale cream rounded-square background.
-   Use orange, golden yellow, cream, warm grey, and dark
    brown/near-black outlines.
-   Keep the composition simple and legible at small application-icon
    sizes.
-   Avoid text, lettering, and unnecessary decorative detail.

The paw should remain the dominant shape, while the dust bunny's face
gives the icon its personality.

### Dust-bunny character

The dust bunny should be a scruffy, fluffy, grey-white little creature
with a comic presence. The desired reference is the general feel of the
dust-bunny character from the *User Friendly* webcomic, but the artwork
should be an original interpretation rather than a direct reproduction.

The current eye design---two large, round, startled eyes---is not quite
right. The character should look **distressed, worried, or
inconvenienced**, rather than simply surprised.

Useful expression directions to explore:

-   **Worried:** uneven or slightly tilted eyes, raised inner brows.
-   **Distressed:** narrowed or squinting eyes, furrowed brows, a tiny
    tense mouth.
-   **Panicked:** wide eyes but very small pupils, with an anxious
    expression.
-   **Grumpy / resigned:** half-lidded eyes, as if the dust bunny knows
    it has been caught.

The preferred direction is distressed and slightly pathetic: the poor
dust bunny has been discovered and pinned by the cat.

### Surprise / impact markers

Keep the small comic-style surprise or impact marks near the paw and
dust bunny. They were liked in the earlier concept and should not be
removed.

However, make them **subtle and less prominent** than the paw and dust
bunny. They should blend into the beige background and act as small
supporting accents, not bold black dots or major graphic elements.

## Overall creative direction

The product identity should combine:

-   Practical filesystem and repository housekeeping.
-   Safe, deliberate decisions about what to preserve, ignore, or
    remove.
-   A playful, slightly absurd cat-versus-dust-bunny metaphor.
-   A warm, simple, distinctive visual style.

The strongest visual idea so far is a ginger Persian-like cat paw
pinning a distressed dust bunny on a beige app-icon background, with
restrained comic impact marks. The strongest naming territory is playful
and invented rather than literal or conventionally technical.

## Next design iteration

Create a refined standalone app icon based on the large left-hand
composition:

1.  Keep the beige rounded-square background and orange fluffy paw.
2.  Keep the dust bunny visibly underneath the paw.
3.  Restore the small impact/surprise markers, but make them muted and
    low-contrast.
4.  Explore several distressed dust-bunny eye and brow expressions.
5.  Keep the artwork clean and bold enough to remain recognizable at
    small sizes.
6.  Do not add text or a wordmark yet.
